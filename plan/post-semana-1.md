## Texto del post (≈200 palabras)

I'm an economist. [X] months ago I had never shipped a line of code to production.

Today I run three products. One of them charges real money, in Swedish kronor, to real sole traders.

It's called Revisor App. Bookkeeping for enskild firma, the Swedish one-person business. Receipts come in, you approve them, they get booked. Every week it tells the owner one thing: one truth, one next action.

Why would an economist build accounting software?

Because I kept seeing the same problem. People who run a one-person business in Sweden are good at their work and bad at their numbers. Not because they're careless. Because the tools assume you're an accountant. [Una frase sobre dónde viste esto: un cliente, un amigo, tu propia firma.]

My training taught me three questions: what can be measured, what incentives apply, what the evidence supports. I build software the same way. Measure first. Show the data. Never dress an opinion as insight.

I build alone, with AI as my engineering team. Product, design, code, payments, deployment. [Una frase sobre cómo fue aprender a hacerlo: una cosa concreta que te costó.]

Next week I'll show how the VAT buffer works, and why it's the feature people ask about most.

What's the part of running your business that you dread most? Tell me. It might be the next thing I build.
